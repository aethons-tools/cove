= Waiting for a reply (wake-on)
:summary: The wake-on engine — how a studio waits after its turn, is paused after `warm-timeout`, and is woken when a squawk reaches it; the `WaitSeq` reply trigger, who wakes in each kind of conversation, mentions, the agent-to-agent limit and its Resume, and the `runtime.wake` and `runtime.agent-wakes` serve config.
:read_when: You need to know why a waiting studio did or did not wake on a reply, or which sessions a room's or work log's message wakes, or when a waiting studio is paused; you are tuning `runtime.wake` (poll-interval, warm-timeout) or `runtime.agent-wakes`; or sessions stopped waking each other.
:owns: wake-on — waiting, pausing at `warm-timeout` and waking on a reply, the `WaitSeq` trigger, who wakes per conversation kind and mentions, the agent-to-agent limit and Resume, and the `runtime.wake` and `runtime.agent-wakes` config
:prereqs: intercom.adoc for the tools, the channel log and the relays; turn-end.adoc for how a turn ends
:tier: leaf
:updated: 2026-11-14

A raised studio is not one-shot. When its agent's turn ends (typically after asking
a question via `send`), the studio *suspends* — it reports Activity `waiting` and
blocks instead of ending (xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc[turn-end.adoc]). Jam's resident *wake-on engine*
watches the studio's inbox and, when a reply arrives, *wakes* it
over the Attach stream; the studio runs its next turn — written into the live agent if one is running, else a new `+claude --continue+` episode — ``read``s the
reply, and resumes. When its xref:usage/jam/session-context.adoc#refresh[session context] changed
meanwhile, the wake text says so. The engine reacts *on signal*: every append to
the channel log and every activity a studio reports runs a check about 200ms later
(a burst coalesces into one), so a reply typically wakes its studio in well under a
second; `poll-interval` is only the backstop for alarms, deadlines and a missed
signal. *No session is torn down for waiting*, whatever started it: a session
waiting for help keeps its studio and its work (it is still paused at
`warm-timeout` and woken on a reply). The role's
xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc#idle-timeout[idle timeout], `end`, and operators end sessions;
xref:usage/jam/escalation.adoc[escalation] draws attention to one that is stuck. `wait-max` is
deprecated and ignored (serve warns when it is set). A personal session instead climbs the
xref:usage/jam/personal-idle-ladder.adoc[idle ladder] — nags into its channel, and an
optional reclaim. Its starter's `keep`/`release` reply to a nag is acted on by Jam
and doesn't wake the agent (xref:usage/jam/personal-idle-ladder.adoc[]).
A standing session has no owner, so it gets no nags.

A reply that lands while the studio is still *``running``* or
*xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc#holding[`holding`]* also wakes it, at once. The Wake is written
straight into the live agent between turns (mid-turn, it is coalesced into one
resume prompt at turn end), and it says why the agent was woken
(xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc#wake-reasons[wake reasons]). A running or holding studio is never
paused or torn down for this.

While waiting, a studio doesn't stay live-and-idle indefinitely: once it's been waiting
past a *``warm-timeout``* with no reply, the engine *pauses* it (`docker pause`, ≈0
CPU; not while a tunnel is open, an alarm's xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc#gates[gate] is running, or it xref:usage/jam/structured-turn-end.adoc#kept-processes[keeps processes] running) and moves it to the `idled` xref:usage/jam/coves.adoc#the-model[phase] — a paused, intentionally
idle studio whose lease-reaping is suspended (see xref:usage/jam/coves.adoc[coves] for the phase
chain). When a reply then lands, the engine *unpauses* it (`Resume`) and sends `Wake`
on the next check once it's reconnected and reporting Live + `waiting` again — the
unpause and the studio's reconnect report each trigger one, so the wake follows the
unpause promptly rather than a poll later.
An open SSH connection holds this off (xref:usage/jam/collaboration.adoc#idle-studios[collaboration]).

The engine always runs and is configured under ``runtime.wake``:

[source,yaml]
----
runtime:
  wake:
    poll-interval: 15s   # backstop sweep of waiting studios; replies wake on signal (default 15s)
    warm-timeout: 5m     # how long a waiting cove stays live before it's paused (default 60s)
----

Each field resolves independently: *``runtime.wake`` > the matching `runtime.requisitioner`
field* (`wake-poll-interval` / `warm-timeout`, kept as a fallback for
existing configs) *> the default*. An invalid `runtime.wake` duration fails `serve`
at startup; an invalid Requisitioner value still falls back to the default.

The wake trigger is *a squawk delivered to the studio* — from a person, an
account, or another session — after a `WaitSeq`
baseline: when a run starts (at raise, and
whenever the studio enters `running`) the supervisor stamps `WaitSeq` to the Log's
current tail sequence — the starting agent reads its inbox itself — and any later
delivery (append `seq` > `WaitSeq`) counts as a reply. Waking a `running` studio advances `WaitSeq` past the replies it was woken for;
entering `waiting` leaves `WaitSeq` alone, so a reply that landed during the run and was
not yet woken for wakes the studio as soon as it waits. It is an append-sequence compare,
not a wall-clock one (`WaitingSince` is unchanged, but now drives only
`warm-timeout` pausing, not reply-detection). It's fed
by the relay ingress engine (xref:usage/jam/intercom.adoc#enabling-it[intercom]), so a Waiting studio wakes on a reply only once a relay
has ingested it; with no relay configured, it waits until the idle timeout or an
operator ends it (pausing at `warm-timeout` still happens).

[#who-wakes]
*Who wakes depends on the conversation's kind.*
Every member gets a message in its inbox,
but only some members wake for it
(`wakes` in `internal/wakeon`):

* In a `dm`, a `group`, or a session channel,
every session member but the author wakes.
* In a `room`,
only the session members the message mentions,
plus those whose membership's wake mode is `all`.
* In a `work` log,
only the unit's current worker (`Channel.Worker`),
unless it's the author.
A session called into the log doesn't wake for its messages.

A call-in notice always wakes the session it calls in.
A message in a conversation Jam doesn't know,
such as a legacy squawk, wakes as before.
The others read the message at their next `read`.

A *mention* names a member of the conversation.
It comes from `send`'s `mentions` field,
a list of `+session:<label|id>+` targets that each name a session member
(`400` otherwise),
or from an `@<label>` in the body that names a member:
a session by its label or id, or a person by their name.
An `@` that follows a letter, such as in an email address, isn't a mention,
and an author never mentions itself.
Jam resolves mentions when it posts the message
and stores them as its mentions part
(`application/vnd.jam.mentions+json`, `+{"ids": [...]}+`),
so a later rename doesn't change who a message named
(`withMentions` in `internal/jam`).
Push reads the people it names
(xref:usage/jam/push.adoc#what-notifies[push]).

[#agent-limit]
*Sessions wake sessions, up to the agent-to-agent limit.*
Another session's post wakes a waiting studio,
in every kind of channel,
until the limit trips.
It trips for a session's post when the 8 posts before it in its channel
are all from sessions,
all after the channel's last *Resume*,
and all within 10 minutes of it
(`loopTripped` and `limitReached` in `internal/wakeon`).
Past it, session posts there are still delivered, and read at the next `read`,
but wake no session.
Jam posts no notice;
the session view shows the tripped limit
(xref:usage/jam/session-activity.adoc#_resume[session-activity]).

The limit lifts in three ways:

* The window slides past: the channel is quiet for long enough.
* A person or an account posts in the channel.
* Someone presses *Resume* in the session view
(`+POST /app/user/api/conversations/{id}/resume+`).
It's allowed to a person who may open the view of one of the channel's sessions:
the operators, or a personal session's starter.
Anyone else gets 404.

*Resume* restarts the count:
posts from before it never count toward the next trip,
and a post the limit held then wakes its session.
Jam stores only the time of a channel's last *Resume* (`Channel.ResumedAt`).
Each held post's verdict is cached by its seq until the next *Resume*.

The numbers are `runtime.agent-wakes`:
`count` (default 8; `0` turns session wakes off) and `window` (default `10m`).
The old key `runtime.wake.session-wake-limit` still sets the count, with a warning;
setting both is an error.

A session's notices — its nags, its "ended" notice,
and the notices the old loop breaker posted — are for people and never wake another session;
a session calling another in does.

A Project may also configure an *escalation policy* that calls ordered tiers of
people into a waiting session's channel on their own per-tier timers when it asks
for a person, instead of leaving it to wait passively — a separate, independent clock from the pause and idle timeout above; see
xref:usage/jam/escalation.adoc[escalation].

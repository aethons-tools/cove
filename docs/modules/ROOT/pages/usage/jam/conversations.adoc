== Why

A session's own channel made every session a broadcaster:
its final text went to everyone in its channel,
so sessions that shared people heard, and answered, messages meant for others.
A ticket channel tied the work's record to an intercom channel,
which ends up doing three jobs: the record, the tracker mirror, and a way to reach the worker.
This model gives each job its own place.

[#kinds]
== Conversation kinds

Every message of the channel log is in one conversation.
A conversation has a kind:

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |What it is

|`dm`
|Exactly two members: two people, a person and a session, or two sessions.
Jam keeps one `dm` for each pair.

|`group`
|Three or more members, fixed at creation.
One `group` for each set of members.

|`room`
|A project's named room, as today.
People and sessions can be members.

|`work`
|The log of one unit of work.
Its members are the current worker, swapped as the unit is dispatched,
and the people who follow it: who posted in it or clicked *Join*.
|===

The `studio` kind (session channels) goes.
Today's chats become `dm` or `group` by their member count.

[#work]
== Units of work

A unit of work is one tracker issue as Jam sees it:
the issue, its sessions (the current one and past ones), its pull requests, its state, and its log.
The Requisitioner creates it when it first raises a session on the issue,
and a re-dispatched issue's new session takes over the same unit.

* *The current worker* is the live session that holds the issue now.
At most one session holds it at a time.
* *The log* is a `work` conversation bound to the issue.
When the unit gets a new worker, Jam removes the old one from its members and adds the new one.
The tracker relay mirrors it both ways, as it mirrors a ticket channel today:
every entry becomes a comment, and every comment on the issue becomes an entry.
* `report` posts its summary to the log,
and asks and whiteboards can live there.

[#targets]
== Targets

[%header,cols="2,3"]
|===
|Target |Goes to

|`+user:<name>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and that person.

|`+session:<label>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and that session.

|`+chat:<member>,<member>…+`
|The `dm` or `group` with exactly those members and the sender.
Members are `+user:+` or `+session:+` targets.

|`+channel:<room>+`
|The room.

|`+work:<key>+`
|The unit of work's log.
`+ticket:<key>+` stays as an alias.

|`+worker:<key>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and the unit's current worker.
With no current worker, `409` with `+{"error": "no-worker"}+`;
nothing is held for a later session.
|===

Addressing is checked as today (xref:usage/jam/comms-addressing.adoc[comms-addressing]),
with two changes:

* `+work:+` and `+worker:+` match a role's `+ticket:+` globs, so existing roles keep working.
* There is no home channel, so no send skips the check,
except a reply: a session may always send to a conversation it was delivered a message in.
A session may always send to its own unit's log (`+work:<its key>+`),
and a personal session to the person who started it.

`send` without `to` goes to the conversation the session itself last sent to in this turn.
The first `send` of a turn needs a `to` (`400` without one).
Incoming messages never change the default, so one arriving mid-turn can't redirect a send.

[#wake]
== Who wakes

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |A new message wakes

|`dm`, `group`
|Every session member but the author.

|`room`
|Only the session members it mentions,
plus the session members that joined with `wake: all`.
The others get it in their inbox without waking.

|`work`
|The current worker, unless it is the author.
People members are notified, not woken.
|===

*Sessions in rooms.*
Today a room refuses session members (`ErrFixedMembers`); the goal lifts that guard.
A session is a member of a room in two ways:

* Its role lists the room in its file,
`scope.rooms: [{name: ops, wake: named}]`,
and its sessions join it when they're raised.
`wake` is `named` (the default) or `all`.
* A member of the room calls it in (`call_in`), and the caller's addressing allows the session.
It joins with `wake: named`.

A session leaves a room it was called into with `leave`;
it can't leave a room its role lists.
A room's sessions show in its member list, marked as agents.

A *mention* is the new `mentions` field of `send` (a list of `+session:+` targets),
or an `@<label>` in the body that names a session member of the room.
People are notified by the push rules (xref:usage/jam/push.adoc[push]).
For a `dm` or `group`, every person member is notified.
For a room or a work log, only the people a message names are notified.

[#rate-limit]
== The agent-to-agent limit

Today's loop breaker applies in every kind of conversation:
after a run of messages authored only by sessions
(8 in a row today, its existing setting),
the next session messages in that conversation are delivered but wake no session.
A person's message in the conversation ends the run.
Jam tells the operators,
and for a personal session the person who started it,
once per run.

In a `dm`, a run is per pair.
A loop among three agents trips it too,
because each of their conversations sees only session messages.
The breaker keeps no state beyond the log.

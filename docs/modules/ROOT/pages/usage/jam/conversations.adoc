== Why

A session's own channel made every session a broadcaster:
its final text went to everyone in its channel,
so sessions that shared people heard, and answered, messages meant for others.
A ticket channel tied the work's record to an intercom channel,
which ends up doing three jobs: the record, the tracker mirror, and a way to reach the worker.
This model gives each job its own place.

[#kinds]
== Conversation kinds

Every message of the channel log is in one conversation.
A conversation has a kind:

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |What it is

|`dm`
|Exactly two members: two people, a person and a session, or two sessions.
Jam keeps one `dm` for each pair.

|`group`
|Three or more members, fixed at creation.
One `group` for each set of members.

|`room`
|A project's named room, as today.
People and sessions can be members.

|`work`
|The log of one unit of work.
It has no members; who gets its entries is a rule (<<wake>>).
|===

The `studio` kind (session channels) goes.
Today's chats become `dm` or `group` by their member count.

[#work]
== Units of work

A unit of work is one tracker issue as Jam sees it:
the issue, its sessions (the current one and past ones), its pull requests, its state, and its log.
The Requisitioner creates it when it first raises a session on the issue,
and a re-dispatched issue's new session takes over the same unit.

* *The current worker* is the live session that holds the issue now.
At most one session holds it at a time.
* *The log* is a `work` conversation bound to the issue.
The tracker relay mirrors it both ways, as it mirrors a ticket channel today:
every entry becomes a comment, and every comment on the issue becomes an entry.
* `report` posts its summary to the log,
and asks and whiteboards can live there.

[#targets]
== Targets

[%header,cols="2,3"]
|===
|Target |Goes to

|`+user:<name>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and that person.

|`+session:<label>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and that session.

|`+chat:<member>,<member>…+`
|The `dm` or `group` with exactly those members and the sender.
Members are `+user:+` or `+session:+` targets.

|`+channel:<room>+`
|The room.

|`+work:<key>+`
|The unit of work's log.
`+ticket:<key>+` stays as an alias.

|`+worker:<key>+`
|The `dm` between the sender and the unit's current worker.
With no current worker, `409` with `+{"error": "no-worker"}+`;
nothing is held for a later session.
|===

Addressing is checked as today (xref:usage/jam/comms-addressing.adoc[comms-addressing]),
with two changes:

* `+work:+` and `+worker:+` match a role's `+ticket:+` globs, so existing roles keep working.
* There is no home channel, so no send skips the check.
A session may always send to its own unit's log (`+work:<its key>+`)
and to its owner.

`send` without `to` replies in the conversation of the newest message the turn was shown.
On a turn with no messages, such as an alarm, `send` needs a `to` (`400`).

[#wake]
== Who wakes

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |A new message wakes

|`dm`, `group`
|Every session member but the author.

|`room`
|Only the session members it mentions,
plus the members whose role sets `wake-on-all` for that room.
The others get it in their inbox without waking.

|`work`
|The current worker, unless it is the author.
|===

A *mention* is the `mentions` field of `send` (a list of `+session:+` targets),
or an `@<label>` in the body that names a session member of the room.
A role opts in per room in its file:
`scope.rooms: [{name: ops, wake: all}]`.

People are notified by the push rules (xref:usage/jam/push.adoc[push]),
with "a member of the channel" read as "a member of the `dm` or `group`",
and "named" for rooms and work logs.

[#turn-end]
== The turn-end rule

Every turn ends in one of two ways:

* It sent something: a `send`, an `ask`, a `report`, or `turn_end` with a `to`.
* It said no message is needed: `turn_end` with `+no_reply: true+`.

Text is thinking unless it's sent.
The final text and mid-turn text are never auto-posted;
`turn_end` with a `to` posts the final text there.

A turn that ends with neither is *returned* once:
Jam wakes the session at once with a note that the turn sent nothing and didn't say no message was needed,
and shows it the same messages again (they aren't committed).
If that turn also ends with neither,
Jam commits, records `unaddressed` on the turn in the session view,
and doesn't return it again.

Interrupted, errored, and `/compact` turns are exempt, as today.
`turn_end` keeps `post: false`, read as `+no_reply: true+`.

[#session-view]
== The session view

The session view is read-only over the log and the Thinking feed:

* *Activity*: every message the session authored or was delivered, from any kind,
with its conversation.
`+GET /app/user/api/sessions/{id}/activity+`, paged like `read`.
* *Thinking*: the existing feed (xref:usage/jam/session-view.adoc[session-view]),
folded between messages a turn at a time, collapsed by default.
Thinking is never indexed for search.
* *Composer*: posts to `+session:<label>+` as the viewer, a `dm`.

*Who may open it:* the session's owner and the Jam's operators.
The owner of a personal session is its starter;
of any other session, the role's owners.
Everyone else gets `404`.
Thinking can quote what a tool printed,
so this is the transcript's access rule, not the conversation's.

After the session ends, the view stays, but without its header or pane.

[#rate-limit]
== The agent-to-agent rate limit

Jam counts the messages one session sends to another,
in any `dm`, `group`, or room mention,
per pair of sessions over a sliding window.
Past the limit, Jam holds the pair's next messages to each other,
notifies both sessions' owners,
and releases the hold when an owner says so or the window passes.
The numbers are serve config, `runtime.agent-rate`.
Today's loop breaker (8 messages in a row between sessions) becomes this limit.

[#escalation]
== Escalation

A tier opens a `dm` between the session and the tier's one person,
or a `group` of the session and the tier's people,
and posts the escalation notice there.
When the session works on a unit,
the notice is also posted to its log.
Tier timing doesn't change (xref:usage/jam/escalation.adoc[escalation]).

[#migration]
== Moving over

* Each live session channel is archived.
Its history stays readable in the session view.
* Each ticket channel becomes its unit's log and keeps its history and its binding to the issue.
* Each chat becomes a `dm` or a `group`.
* `+session:<label>+`, `+ticket:<key>+`, and `+chat:+` keep working as above,
so role files and context pages need no change at the switch.
* A personal session's starter gets a `dm` with it;
the idle ladder's nags go there.
* The Discord relay posts `dm` and `group` messages to people's inbox channels,
as it posts chats today.

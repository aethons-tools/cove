= The structured turn end
:summary: The structured turn end — a woken session finds its pending messages in its prompt, its text is thinking unless it sends it (the turn-end rule: a turn that ends with text and sent nothing is returned once), and what it was shown is committed for it; the optional `turn_end` tool changes those defaults (recipient, no reply, deny, compact, end, mute, wake time, idle timeout), and `keep_*` keeps processes running across turns. Covers the turn-protocol switch, `PUT /mute`, the auto-post marker and the agent socket.
:read_when: You want to know what a woken agent is shown, why a session's reply was (or wasn't) posted, why a turn was returned or marked unaddressed, or why its messages were committed, how an agent keeps a preview server running between turns, or you are changing the turn-end defaults, `turn_end`/`keep_*`, or how a studio and Jam agree on the turn protocol.
:owns: the structured turn end — inbox batches in the wake prompt (caps, rendering, fallback), the turn-end rule (posting the final text to `turn_end`'s `to`, returned turns and the `returned` wake, `unaddressed`), auto-commit, the per-turn send record and `send`'s default `to`, the `turn_end` tool and its directives, `PUT /mute`, `PUT /kept`, the auto-post marker part and wake-on's skip of it, the duplicate-reply skip, the turn-protocol switch (`GET /context?turn-protocol=3`, `Bundle.turn_protocol`), the agent socket, kept processes (`keep_start`/`keep_stop`/`keep_list`) and the `process-exited` wake. Does NOT own wake-on's other rules (intercom.md), alarms/idle/`end` (turn-end.md) or the context compile (session-context.md).

An agent loop ends each turn with a message. Under the structured turn end, Jam uses that instead of making the agent do its messaging in tool calls:

* *The wake brings the messages.* The agent's prompt carries its pending messages.
* *Text is thinking unless it's sent.* Nothing the agent writes is posted for it:
it answers with `send`, `ask`, or `report`,
or ends with `turn_end` and a `to`, which posts its final text there
(<<the-turn-end-rule,the turn-end rule>>).
* *Handled is the default.* What the agent was shown is committed for it.

A plain question and answer then costs one model call, not a `read` → answer → `send` → `commit` round. The design is in the https://github.com/aethons-tools/cove/blob/main/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-structured-turn-end-design.md[spec].

== When it is on

It is on per studio, and only when both sides speak it:

* *The studio asks for it.* The studio's jam-studio-agent fetches its context with `GET /context?turn-protocol=3` (the turn-end rule).
* *Jam confirms it.* A Jam that knows the protocol compiles the matching guidance and answers with `+"turn_protocol": 3+` in the bundle. It also records the protocol on the Instance, so setup inspection recompiles the same context, and so `send` knows to refuse a missing `to` (<<send-default,below>>).
* *An older studio* asks for `2`, whose cove still posts its final text for it.
Jam keeps answering `2` with that protocol's guidance
("End your turn with your reply"),
and keeps a `send` with no `to` going to its home channel.
* *Otherwise it stays off.* A studio without a live context refresh, or a Jam that answers without the protocol it asked for, keeps the legacy behavior: the read prompt, explicit `send`/`commit`, and the legacy guidance. This prevents a Jam and a studio that disagree from duplicating replies (or the reverse, losing them).

== Delivery: messages in the wake

* *Every wake carries a batch.* Whatever woke the session (a squawk, an alarm, the idle timeout, a kept process), its prompt carries its pending inbox entries:
** the entries are past the commit cursor (xref:usage/jam/intercom.adoc#the-inbox-as-a-durable-queue[intercom]), oldest first;
** the batch is capped at *20 entries or 32 KiB* of rendered text.
* *What an entry looks like.* Each entry is a header line, `+From <author> in <channel> (<kind>) at <time> — id <id>+`, followed by its body. Each attachment adds an `+[attachment <name> ref <ref>]+` line.
* *Oversized entries.* An entry alone over the cap is cut, with a marker telling the agent to `read` that id for the rest. A cut entry isn't committed for the agent: it commits it itself once handled, and until then auto-commit doesn't pass it.
* *The closing line* says either "Reply with `send` … or end with `turn_end` and a `to`…" or, when entries were left out, "More messages are waiting — use `read`…".
* *Fetched just before writing.* The batch is fetched (`GET /squawks`) just before the prompt is written into the episode. Because it starts from the commit cursor at that moment, it never re-shows messages the agent already read and committed itself.
* *Fallback.* If the fetch fails, that wake gets the legacy read prompt.
* *Interrupts and restarts.* The interrupt acknowledgement and a standing session's restart prompt carry the batch too.

== At turn end: post, commit, apply

The studio reads each turn's `result` event and then acts in this order. It finishes before it writes anything else into the episode.

. *Post or return* by <<the-turn-end-rule,the turn-end rule>>.
. *Commit.* It commits the batch the turn's prompt showed (`POST /squawks/commit`), up to the last entry shown in full.
** *Only that turn's batch.* Each prompt is numbered, and only the turn that answers it commits its batch. A turn a background task started commits nothing.
** *Not when it's returned,* nor after a failed post: the messages come back.
. *Apply the turn's directives* (below).

A turn that didn't end normally posts, commits, returns, and applies nothing: an interrupt, an error result, or a process that died. Its directives are dropped, as are those of a turn that never reached its end. A `/compact` turn (a press or `+turn_end+`) posts, commits, and returns nothing either.

[#the-turn-end-rule]
== The turn-end rule

A turn ends in one of four ways:

* *Text and a destination:* `turn_end` with a `to` posts the trimmed final text there,
as the session, marked as a turn end's (`POST /squawks` with `+auto: true+`).
A `send`, `ask`, or `report` during the turn counts the same:
the final text then stays thinking.
* *Text and no message needed:* `turn_end` with `+no_reply: true+` (or `post: false`).
The text stays thinking.
* *No text:* nothing is posted, and the turn isn't returned.
* *Text with no destination:* the turn is *returned*.

Mid-turn text (what the model writes before a tool call) is never posted.
It shows only in the xref:usage/jam/session-view.adoc#thinking[Thinking] tab.

*A returned turn.*
jam-studio-agent commits nothing
and wakes the session at once with the reason `returned`
(a local wake, like `process-exited`; Jam isn't asked).
Its line says the turn sent nothing and didn't say no message was needed,
and the prompt shows the same messages again.
`returned` is a per-turn flag: the prompt it causes answers a returned turn.
If that turn also ends with text and no destination,
it is committed, not returned again,
and recorded as `unaddressed` in the session event stream
(`+{"type":"cove","subtype":"turn","outcome":"unaddressed"}+`),
which the Thinking tab shows.
A turn that ends the session (`next: end`) isn't returned.

*A refused `to`.*
If the post to `to` fails (refused or unreachable),
the reply reached no one:
nothing is committed,
and the turn is returned with a line that says why.
A returned turn's failed post isn't returned again;
its messages stay pending, and the next prompt opens with why.

*How the studio knows what a turn sent.*
After a successful `send`, `ask`, or `report`,
the intercom MCP server tells the agent socket (`POST /turn/sent`, with a send's `to`).
The record is cleared when the next prompt is written, and at each turn end.

[#send-default]
*`send` without `to`.*
The MCP server asks the agent socket (`GET /turn`) where the turn's last `send` went,
and sends there.
A turn's first `send` needs a `to`:
without one, the tool fails before it reaches Jam.
Jam enforces the same rule for a session on protocol 3:
`POST /squawks` with no `to` is a `400` ("send needs a `to`").
Incoming messages never change the default.
Asks and whiteboards keep their default, the home channel.

*Auto-posts don't wake sessions.* A turn end's post carries the part `pass:c[application/vnd.jam.turn-end+json]`. Wake-on skips it when its author is a session (xref:usage/jam/wake-on.adoc[wake-on]), and it never counts toward the agent-to-agent limit. People are notified as usual. A session that wants another session to act uses `send`.

*Duplicate replies.* Jam skips an auto-post whose trimmed body equals a `send` by the same session in the same channel since its previous auto-post, within 10 minutes. It answers `+{"skipped": true}+`, which covers an agent that also ``send``s its reply. The same short answer at two turn ends is two replies, and both are posted.

[#turn_end]
== `+turn_end+`

`+turn_end+` is an intercom tool, called before the final message, only when the defaults don't fit. The last call in a turn wins, and it applies to that turn only.

[%header]
|===
|Field |Default |Effect

|`to` |— |where the final text is posted (``send``'s targets); without it, the final text is thinking
|`+no_reply+` |false |true: no message is needed; the text stays thinking and the turn isn't returned
|`post` |true |false: read as `+no_reply: true+`
|`commit` |true |false (deny): the shown messages stay pending and come back with the next wake. Deny alone doesn't wake.
|`next` |`wait` |`compact` (compact, then wait) or `end` (with ``reason``: the `end` tool's effect)
|`+wake_on_message+` |true |false: `PUT /mute`. New messages don't wake the session until it next runs. Alarms, the idle timeout and kept processes still do, and an owner's keep/release reply to a nag still acts. The next turn end with the default unmutes.
|`+wake_at+` |— |``+{at, note?, name?}+``: a one-shot alarm (`PUT /alarms/<name>`, default name `turn-end`)
|`+idle_timeout+` |role's |as `+idle_timeout(…, scope: next)+`
|===

`+alarm_set+`, `+idle_timeout+` and `end` still work mid-turn (xref:usage/jam/turn-end.adoc[]).

[#kept-processes]
== Kept processes

A background process the agent starts in a shell outlives its turn (claude exits at a quiet turn end, and the process is reparented to init), but it freezes while the studio is paused, and nothing watches or stops it. `+keep_start(name, command, workdir?)+` starts a process that keeps running while the session waits, owned by jam-studio-agent itself (the long-lived Workload), not by the agent CLI.

* *How it runs.* It runs as `sh -c` in its own process group, with the agent's environment, and starts in the workspace unless `workdir` says otherwise.
* *Output* goes to `/agent-data/keep/<name>.log`. The log is capped at 1 MiB, with one `.1` rotation.
* *Limits.* At most 8 at a time. A name is `+[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]{0,31}+`.
* *Stopping one.* `+keep_stop(name)+` sends SIGTERM to the group, then SIGKILL after 10 s. `+keep_list()+` lists them.
* *When it exits on its own,* the session is woken with the reason `process-exited`, rendered as `+Kept process `<name>` exited (status N); last lines:+` plus up to 20 log lines.
* *They stop with the session* (teardown, `end`).
* *When the shell exits,* whatever it left in its process group is killed with it.
* *After a jam-studio-agent restart,* whatever its predecessor kept is stopped (found through `/agent-data/keep/registry.json`), and the first prompt names those processes. A process is stopped only when its boot id and start time match the registry, so a reused pid is never signalled.
* *Not paused.* While any kept process runs, a waiting session isn't paused at `warm-timeout`, so its kept processes keep running. The studio reports the count to Jam (`+PUT /kept {"count": n}+`, recorded as the Instance's `+kept_processes+`). An older Jam ignores it, and the processes then freeze with the pause.

The agent tends what it keeps: its guidance tells it to agree with the person how long a preview should run, and to set a `+wake_at+` to ask them then. Jam imposes no lifetime.

== The agent socket

`+turn_end+` and `+keep_*+` reach the Workload over a unix socket at `/dev/shm/jam-agent.sock` (mode 0600). It is served by jam-studio-agent and used by its MCP subcommand (`jam-studio-agent mcp`). Its routes are:

* ``PUT /turn-end``;
* `POST /turn/sent` and `GET /turn` (what the turn sent, and its last `send`'s `to`);
* `POST /keeps`, `+DELETE /keeps/{name}+` and `GET /keeps`.

`+turn_end+` answers 409 while the structured turn end is off. Against a studio with no socket, either tool fails with "upgrade the studio".

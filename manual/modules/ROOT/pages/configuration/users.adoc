= Configuring users
:page-area: ROOT
:page-id: ROOT/configuration/users
:page-conventions: sentence-case

A *user* is a person that Jam knows:
someone who signs in,
and whom agents can send messages to.
A user is one person on the whole Jam,
even when they are a member of several projects.
You see and change users under *Config › Users*.

The user's name, logins and removal are part of the config repo:
you change them on a branch
(xref:configuration/config-branches.adoc[]),
and each save is a commit to that branch.
OIDC identities, accounts and connected apps change at once,
on any branch, including the followed one.

To add a user to a project,
use the project's *Members* page
(xref:configuration/projects.adoc#_members[Configuring a project]).

== The users list

image::users-list.png[The users list,800]

*Users* lists every user,
with their logins,
their accounts (each with its connection and handle or user id),
and the projects they are a member of.
Click a name to open the user's page.

On a branch, *New user* takes:

* a *Name*, unique on the Jam;
* optional *Logins*, one per line;
* optional *OIDC identities*, one per line, as `issuer:subject`.

*Create user* makes the user and opens their page.

== A user's page

image::user-page.png[alice's page, on a branch,800]

The heading shows the user's name and id.
On a branch, *Remove user* removes them, after you confirm
(<<removing-a-user,Removing a user>>).
*Operator notes* hold notes for operators only.

Name::
On a branch, type a new name and click *Rename*.
Jam refuses while the user owns a running personal agent.
Escalation tiers that name the user by name do not change with it:
update them after a rename
(xref:configuration/projects.adoc#escalation[Escalation]).
Projects::
The projects the user is a member of.
Click one to open it.
Logins::
The sign-in identities of this person in the admin console,
one per line,
such as the subject of their single sign-on login,
or `local` on a Jam you reach on your own machine.
They tell Jam who is behind a request,
for example who owns a personal agent.
On a branch, *Save logins* replaces the whole list.
OIDC identities::
The browser logins for this person in the Inbox,
one per line, as `issuer:subject`.
*Save identities* replaces the whole list.

A login or identity belongs to one person only:
Jam refuses one that another user already has.

[#accounts]
=== Accounts

*Accounts* are this person on the services that Jam is connected to:
their handle on the tracker,
or their Discord user id.
Jam uses them to know who wrote a comment or a reply there,
and to @-mention the person.
Each row shows the *Connection*,
the service's *User id*,
the *Handle*,
and Jam's id for the account.

* *Add account* takes a *Connection*,
the service's *User id* (such as a Discord user id)
and the *Handle* (without the @).
* *Unlink* takes the account away from this user, after you confirm.

=== Connected apps

*Connected apps* lists the apps,
such as VS Code,
that are signed in as this person:
each with when it was last used, when it expires,
and *operator rights* if it has them.
*Revoke* signs the app out at once, after you confirm.

[#removing-a-user]
== Removing a user

*Remove user* takes the person out of every project.
Jam refuses while they own a running personal agent:
release the agent first.
Take them out of any escalation tiers first, too.

Jam keeps a removed user's id,
so messages and history still show them,
as *_name_ (removed)*.
Their page is read-only.
The name is free for a new user.

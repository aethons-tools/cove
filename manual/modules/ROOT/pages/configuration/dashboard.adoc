= The dashboard, search and health
:page-area: ROOT
:page-id: ROOT/configuration/dashboard
:page-conventions: sentence-case

The admin console is the part of the Jam app for admins,
at `/app/admin`
(xref:start/signing-in.adoc[Signing in and finding your way]).
It has four tabs in the title bar:

Dashboard::
The Jam at a glance, and what needs attention.
Config::
Every configured thing, in a tree:
projects, users, kits, destinations and model specs.
Agents::
Every agent, running or not
(xref:configuration/agents.adoc[An agent's page]).
Health::
Problems Jam found in itself,
and the Channel log.

image::title-bar-admin.png[The admin console's tabs, with attention badges, and the search box,800]

== The Config tree

The *Config* pages show a tree on the left.
Click a name to open its page.
Click ▸ to open a group and ▾ to close it.
The tree remembers which groups you opened,
and the group of the page you are on is always open.

image::config-tree.png[The Config tree, open at acme's Escalation,300]

The tree holds:

* *Tracking*: the config repo the Jam follows
(xref:configuration/config-branches.adoc[Editing on a branch and applying the config repo]).
* *Projects*: each project,
with its *Members*, *Agents* (its roles), *Intercom* and *Escalation*
(xref:configuration/projects.adoc[Configuring a project],
xref:configuration/roles.adoc[Configuring roles and raising agents]).
* *Users* (xref:configuration/users.adoc[Configuring users]).
* *Kits* (xref:configuration/kits.adoc[Configuring a kit]).
* *Destinations* (xref:configuration/destination.adoc[Configuring a destination]).
* *Model Specs* (xref:configuration/model-specs.adoc[Configuring a model spec]).

Type in *Filter* above the tree to show only the names that match.
While you filter, every group is open.
The tree refreshes every 3 seconds.

[#search]
== Search

The search box in the title bar looks through the whole Jam at once.
Press `/` anywhere outside a text field to jump to it.
Type at least 2 characters and press Enter.

* If one thing's name is exactly what you typed,
such as an agent's id or `acme/reviewer`,
Jam opens its page.
* Otherwise the *Search* page lists the matches,
in groups.
Each match links to its page.
Type in the box on that page to search again as you type.

image::search.png[Search results for "review",700]

Search finds agents, roles, projects, kits, destinations, model specs, users and rooms,
by name and by what they contain:
an agent's ticket and owner,
a role's kit and destinations,
a kit's prompt and allowed hosts,
a destination's routes and addresses,
or a user's logins and account handles.
It also finds message text:
it shows the 10 newest messages that match,
with a link to the Channel log for the rest.
Case does not matter.

== The dashboard

The *Dashboard* tab is the console's first page.
It refreshes every 3 seconds.

image::dashboard.png[The dashboard,800]

From top to bottom:

* *Tiles* that count the agents by status:
*Running*, *Waiting*, *Setting up*, *Lost / terminating* and *Idled*.
Click one to open the *Agents* tab showing only those agents.
Then come the counts of *Projects*, *Agents*, *Users* and *Specs*
(kits, destinations and model specs together).
All but *Projects* open their list.
* *Needs attention*: problems with the Jam itself
(below).
* *Operator notes*,
when the Jam's own config file has notes for operators.
Agents never see them.
* *Agent context*: what every agent on this Jam is told
(xref:projects:agent-context.adoc[]).
* *Managers*,
when the Jam runs its studios on Studio Managers:
each one's status, version, how many studios it runs and can run,
when it was last seen,
memory and disk use,
how much disk it could free,
its last clean-up,
and what it is doing now, such as building a kit.
The disk bar turns amber at 90% and red at 95%.
* *Running agents*: every agent that has a studio,
with its status,
whether its connector and image are up to date,
its project, role, manager and ticket,
and when Jam last heard from it.
*timeline* opens the agent's live session.

=== Needs attention

*Needs attention* lists the problems that belong to the Jam as a whole,
rather than to one project:

* a problem on the *Health* tab that is critical or a warning,
* a kit whose current version Jam cannot read,
* a destination that clashes with another one for a role.

Each line says what is wrong,
and links to the page where you fix it.
Its tag says what kind of problem it is:
*broken*, *warning* or *config*.
When there is nothing,
the card says *Nothing needs attention.*

Problems in a project,
such as an agent whose studio is out of date,
are not on this card:
the badges show them.

[#attention-badges]
== Attention badges

The console counts everything that needs an admin,
and shows the count as a badge.
A badge is *red* when at least one thing in it is broken,
and *amber* otherwise.
Point at a badge to see what it counts,
such as `1 broken · 1 out of date`.

On the tabs:

* *Agents* counts agents whose studio is broken
(lost, terminating, recovering, or failing to update its network rules)
or out of date (an old image or connector).
* *Config* counts configuration that names something that is not there:
a role whose kit or model spec is gone,
an escalation target who is nobody in the project,
a kit Jam cannot read,
or a destination that clashes with another one.
* *Health* counts the open critical and warning problems.
* *Dashboard* counts all of them.

In the *Config* tree,
a badge counts the configuration problems at that item or below it.
For example, a project's badge adds up its roles' and its escalation's problems.
In the agent tables,
a red or amber *!* next to an agent marks its problem;
point at it to see why.
One agent counts once, as its worst problem.

The tree's badges refresh every 3 seconds.
The tabs' badges refresh each time you open a page.

== The Health tab

*Health* lists the problems Jam has found in itself,
each with how to fix it.
It refreshes every 10 seconds.

image::health.png[The Health tab,800]

*Open* lists the problems that are still there.
Each one shows:

* its severity:
*critical* (agents fail now),
*warning* (something works badly, or soon will),
or *info* (shown here only),
* what is wrong, and for how long,
* how to fix it, in a box you can copy from,
* sometimes *Detail*, which you open for more,
* its key, which names the problem and the thing it is about.

A problem closes by itself when Jam sees the thing work again.
*Resolved (7 days)* lists the ones that closed in the last week,
how long each one lasted,
and when it closed.

Jam raises problems such as:

* a credential it cannot get,
* a pooled account whose token will not refresh,
* push notifications that keep failing,
* a Studio Manager whose disk is nearly full,
* a kit that could not be moved to the new format,
or still uses an old name,
* a destination that sets a name reserved for Jam,
* a config repo that Jam could not apply,
or whose first apply waits for you to confirm it.

If your team gets alerts from Alertmanager,
*Silences ↗* opens its page,
where you can quiet an alert you know about.

== The Channel log

*Channel log*, linked from the top of *Health*,
shows every message in every channel,
newest first.
You can only read it here.

image::channel-log.png[The Channel log,800]

Each row shows when the message was sent,
who sent it,
its channel and project,
and the message.
Click an agent's name to open its page,
or a channel to see only its messages.

To narrow the list,
fill in any of the filters and click *Filter*:

* *Project*.
* *Participant*: the id of a user, an agent, an account or a channel.
* *Body contains*: text in the message.
* *Since* and *Until*: dates, taken as whole days in UTC.
A date Jam cannot read is left out,
and the page says so.

The filters go into the page's address,
so you can share a filtered view as a link.
*Reset* clears them.
The list does not update by itself;
click *Refresh* to see new messages.

If your Jam was upgraded from the old message log,
choose *Legacy* under *Log* to read the messages from before the upgrade.

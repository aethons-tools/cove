= Intercom: conversations, messages, and asks
:page-area: ROOT
:page-id: ROOT/concepts/intercom
:page-conventions: sentence-case

The intercom is how people and agents talk in Jam.
Every message is in one *conversation*,
and the conversation's members get it.
People read and write in the xref:inbox/channels.adoc[Inbox];
agents use their messaging tools.
Both see the same messages.

What an agent works on has a conversation of its own, its work log
(xref:concepts/work.adoc[Units of work]).
What one agent says and thinks across all of its conversations
shows in its xref:inbox/session.adoc[session view].

== Conversations

There are three kinds:

DM::
A conversation between two members: two people, a person and an agent, or two agents.
Each pair has one DM.
Group DM::
A conversation between a fixed group of people and agents.
It's named after the others in it.
Nobody can join or leave a group DM.
Room::
A named conversation of a project, such as `ops`.
People join and leave rooms.
Agents can be members of a room too.

An agent has no conversation of its own.
Everything it says goes to someone:
a person, another agent, a group, a room, or a work log.

The members of a conversation get every message posted in it from the time they joined.
A message you get but haven't read counts as *unread*.

== Who can message whom

You can start a conversation with anyone in your projects:

* Another member of the project, in a DM.
* A group of members, in a group DM.
* A live agent, in a DM.
Someone else's personal session isn't offered.
* A room of the project.
* A unit of work: its log, or the agent working on it
(xref:concepts/work.adoc#_reaching_the_work[Reaching the work]).

An agent can post only where its role allows.
The role's *addressing* lists the people, rooms, agents, and work that the role's agents can message
(xref:configuration/roles.adoc[Configuring roles and raising agents]).

== When agents wake

A message wakes the agents it's for:

* In a DM or a group DM, every agent in it.
* In a room, only the agents the message names, such as `@editor`,
and agents whose role wakes them for every message in that room.
Other agents in the room read the message the next time they wake.
* In a work log, the agent working on it.

Jam limits how often agents wake each other.
When two agents send each other many messages in a short time,
Jam holds their next messages and tells a person.

== Ending a turn

An agent works in turns.
Each turn ends one of two ways:

* The agent sends a message to someone.
* The agent says that no message is needed.

What an agent writes without sending it is thinking:
it shows in the agent's session view, not in any conversation.
If a turn ends with neither,
Jam returns the turn to the agent once to choose,
so an answer is never lost by accident.

== Joining, leaving, and calling in

You join a room when you post in it,
or when you click *Join*.
You can leave a room any time.
What you got before you left stays in the room.

A member of a room can *call in* someone else.
The person or agent joins from then on,
and the room shows a note, such as "alice called in carol."

When an agent asks for help,
Jam opens a DM between it and the people on call
(xref:concepts/escalation.adoc[Escalation]).

== Messages

A message is markdown by default:
the Inbox shows it formatted.
A message sent as *plain text* shows exactly as typed,
which is good for logs.

== Attachments

People and agents can attach files to a message,
such as screenshots, logs, and PDFs.
The Jam operator turns attachments on and sets the size limit
(25 MB per file unless they change it).
A message can have up to 10 files.
Only the members of a conversation can open its files.
Jam shows PNG, JPEG, GIF, and WebP images in the message.
Other files, including HTML and SVG, download.
Files are kept as long as the messages.

== Asks

An agent can *ask* a question with answers to pick from,
in any conversation or work log it can post in.
The ask shows as a card there,
and the conversation moves to *Needs you* in your Inbox
until someone answers
(xref:inbox/asks.adoc[Answering asks]).

== Outside Jam

Some conversations also show on another service:

* A work log is mirrored to its issue in the tracker (Linear or GitHub Issues),
both ways
(xref:concepts/work.adoc[Units of work]).
* A room can be bound to a tracker issue or a Discord channel.
Its messages go there, and posts there come back.
* If the project uses Discord for chat,
Jam posts your DMs and group DMs to your own Discord inbox channel.
To answer, use Discord's reply on the message.
A message in your inbox channel that isn't a reply doesn't go to Jam.

Attachments show only in Jam.

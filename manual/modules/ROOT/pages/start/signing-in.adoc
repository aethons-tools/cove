= Signing in and finding your way
:page-area: ROOT
:page-id: ROOT/start/signing-in
:page-conventions: sentence-case

The Jam app is one web app with two areas.
You sign in once,
and Jam decides which areas you may use.

== Signing in

Open your Jam's address in a browser.
If you are not signed in,
Jam sends you to your company's sign-in page (single sign-on, OIDC).
After you sign in,
Jam sends you back to the app.

Jam then looks at who you are:

* If you are a member of at least one xref:concepts/project.adoc[project],
you are a *member*:
you may use the Inbox.
* If your sign-in gives you operator rights,
you are an *admin*:
you may use the admin console.
* You can be both.

A member lands on the Inbox,
and an admin who is not a member lands on the admin console.
If you are neither,
Jam shows a *Not authorized* page instead.

The *Not authorized* page says why.
It also shows when your sign-in has partly expired,
for example when an admin who is also a member opens the console
after their operator rights lapsed.
Click *Sign in again* to start a new sign-in.

=== Signing in on the Jam's own machine

A browser on the machine that runs Jam
(or one that reaches it through an SSH tunnel)
is always an admin.
It needs no sign-in for the admin console;
the account menu then reads *Local admin*.
The Inbox still needs you to sign in,
because it must know which person you are:
it shows *Sign in to use the Inbox*.
If you sign in there,
you keep your own name and are an admin too.

== The two areas

The Inbox::
At `/app/user`.
Your channels, messages and asks, across all your projects
(xref:inbox/channels.adoc[Reading and replying]).
The admin console::
At `/app/admin`.
Where admins configure the Jam and watch its agents
(xref:configuration/dashboard.adoc[The dashboard, search and health]).

Every page has its own address,
so you can bookmark it or send a link to it.

== The title bar

Every page starts with the same title bar.

image::title-bar-admin.png[The title bar in the admin console,800]

From left to right it shows:

* The Jam's name, such as *Acme Jam*.
* The tabs you may use:
*Inbox* if you are a member,
and *Dashboard*, *Config*, *Agents* and *Health* if you are an admin.
The tab you are on is highlighted.
An admin's tabs can carry a red or amber count of things that need attention
(xref:configuration/dashboard.adoc#attention-badges[Attention badges]).
* In the admin console, the search box
(xref:configuration/dashboard.adoc#search[Search]).
* The theme button.
Each click moves to the next theme:
light, then your device's setting, then dark.
* Your account menu, behind the round button with your initial.
It shows your name and whether you are a member, an admin, or both.
In the Inbox it also holds this device's *Notifications*, *Sounds…* and *Connected apps…*
(xref:inbox/account.adoc[Your account, app tokens and notifications]).
*Sign out* is there too,
except for a local admin who did not sign in.

On a narrow screen,
the *Config* pages put their tree behind the ☰ button.

== Installing the app

You can install the Jam app like a native app,
so it opens in its own window.
Use your browser's own command:
*Install* in Chrome and Edge,
*Add to Dock* in Safari on macOS,
or *Add to Home Screen* on a phone.
The app has no install button of its own.

The installed app is named after your Jam.
It is always online and always current:
it keeps nothing for offline use.
Installing works only when the Jam is served over HTTPS
(or on `localhost`).
On iPhone and iPad,
only the installed app can show notifications and a badge.

== Opening Jam from VS Code

The Jam extension for VS Code (and VSCodium)
puts your conversations in the editor
and opens a session's studio over SSH in one click.

. Install it with `jam vscode install`.
Your Jam serves the extension that matches it.
. Run *Jam: Sign In* in VS Code.
Your browser opens a page that asks *Connect VS Code on …?*.
It names the person you act as.
Click *Allow*.
VS Code then has an app token for you,
which you can revoke later under *Connected apps…*.
. The *Conversations* view in the Jam side bar
lists your conversations, the same way as the Inbox's rail.
Click one to open it as a tab in the *Jam hub*,
a VS Code window just for conversations.
Double-click a session to open its studio window.

To open a session's studio from the web app instead,
use *Open in VS Code* in the session's header
(xref:inbox/session.adoc[Watching a session]).
